Nothing Here Rewards Prescribing

Read the structure closely: you are paid per consult attended, never per script written. A twenty-minute conversation ending in "no, this isn't right for you" pays you exactly what a prescription does. That's the distinction regulators actually look for, and it is the one this model protects.

On consult length, and where the scale actually comes from. We know twenty minutes is the length you want for a new patient, and we're not asking you to give it up. But being honest about the arithmetic on a first consult: asking every question the file requires, waiting for an answer that's actually satisfactory rather than the first thing the patient says, taking proper notes and building a real history, with someone who isn't a chatty Kathy, lands somewhere around eight to sixteen minutes far more often than it lands at twenty. Some patients need the full twenty and more, and should have it. Plenty are genuinely finished at ten, and stretching them out serves nobody. So a new-patient hour realistically settles near 5.5 consults, about $212.50.

The re-scripts are where the shape changes. The history is already on file, the product is either working or it isn't, and the conversation is a genuine review rather than a fresh assessment. Realistically that shouldn't run past six or seven minutes even with a chatty Kathy, which is eight or nine an hour, so $230. Reviews are worth less individually, so it takes five of them rather than three to cover your base, but the volume more than makes up the difference and the hour still clears the sessional rate comfortably. That is the economy of scale in this model, and it lands on your side of the table at least as much as ours. And if a patient needs forty minutes, take forty minutes: that hour pays $150 and nobody will raise it with you.
Yes, more consults means more money. That's ownership, not pressure. We'll say it plainly rather than have you find it: past three consults an hour, your earnings do go up. But that upside sits above a dedicated salary that is already yours unconditionally. It isn't a productivity bonus dangled in front of a wage. It's the ordinary consequence of owning nearly half a business: when there's capacity in your day, you have the option to generate additional revenue for the clinic and for yourself at the same time, because they're the same balance sheet. An option is not an obligation. Nothing in this structure ever requires you to trade away integrity, safety or a proper consultation to take it up, and the guardrails hold whatever the pay curve says: minimum consultation times, daily load caps, and a clinic whose entire commercial position rests on being the careful one. A book of rushed consults damages the asset you own far more than an extra $25 improves your afternoon. And at no point does any of it pay you for a script, which is the thing AHPRA and the TGA are actually auditing.

Medico-Legal Protection

We Protect Your Registration Like It's Ours

A prescriber's most valuable asset is their registration, and if you take equity in this business, its compliance record becomes your asset too. Both are the reason the framework below was designed before the first patient rather than after the first audit. It is the single area where we have been least willing to move fast.

Audit-Ready Documentation

Structured consult notes, documented clinical justifications for every patient, and templates that capture what an auditor would look for, so the record always backs the decision.

Minimum Consult Times & Load Caps

No three-minute scripts. Minimum consultation times are set per consult type, because a first assessment and a monthly review are not the same piece of work, and daily patient-load caps sit over the top of both. Neither moves for the pay curve, so you're never pressured into a rushed or volume-driven decision.

Mandatory Follow-Up & Outcomes

Scheduled, nurse-coordinated follow-ups and outcome tracking are built into the model, demonstrating ongoing care rather than one-off prescribing.

Real-Time Monitoring, Every State

Every patient's history is checked in their state's real-time prescription monitoring system before a Schedule 8 cannabis product is prescribed, and the check is recorded in the note. For your WA patients that's ScriptCheckWA; interstate it's SafeScript, QScript and the ERRCD as applicable. Monitoring is never the gap in one of our files.

State S8 Authorisations, Handled

The most common compliance miss in this sector is forgetting the state layer on top of TGA approval. We hold it in every state we treat. In WA specifically, authorisation is required above the daily-THC thresholds (>40mg oral, >300mg inhaled) and for patients under 18, with prior substance use, or with a significant psychiatric history. Those triggers are checked per patient inside the workflow, not remembered.
