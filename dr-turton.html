Part One

We Did Not Advertise. We Went Looking for You.

One reason, and it is the whole of it: how you practise. We went looking for it specifically, and it is a very short list.

🌿

You Take the Time. That Is the Whole Reason.

Sleep, breath, nutrition, movement, and real work on trauma and addiction, with cannabis as one instrument rather than the product being sold. You spend the consultation on the cause underneath the symptom, and you say no when no is the answer. We are not asking you to become a cannabis doctor. We are asking you to keep being exactly the doctor you already are, somewhere the economics finally pay for it.

🤝

Led by First Nations People

Bugalwah is led by First Nations people. Its director, Cindy Roberts, is First Nations, and the service is being designed with community rather than around it. That governance is settled and it is not the job we are offering you. What sits beside it, and what the clinic does not yet have, is a clinician the people walking through that door can afford to be unhurried with.

⚕

You Are Already Approved

You hold Authorised Prescriber status. A clinic that hires an unapproved doctor spends its first month in a TGA queue before it can treat anybody. With you, day one is day one. It also means the scope conversation is a real one, because you already know what the approval does and does not cover.

Unhurried is not a perk here. It is the design. You cannot cover sleep architecture, breathing patterns and a trauma history in a seven minute slot. Because the hourly is paid whatever the hour holds, a forty five minute consultation costs this clinic exactly what a five minute one does, so there is no financial reason anywhere in this business to hurry you, no per script anything, and no volume target waiting further down the page. Consultation length goes back to being a clinical decision, which is the only thing it ever should have been.
And it is the whole reason we called you. The sector spent five years rewarding speed and is being audited for exactly that. The correction favours doctors who were never in that game, and there are not many of them, because practising carefully has cost money everywhere else. We would rather build a clinic around one of them and give them a real stake in it than hire fast now and apologise for it later. On the evidence of how you practise, that is you.
What you would actually run. Bugalwah is the front door: free first consult through the referral line, telehealth so distance stops deciding who gets seen, and a nursing team trained so nobody feels judged for turning up. Golden Ratio sits behind it, a national book cleared in every state, $49 to be seen and $29 to renew, patients from every walk of life. One roster, one standard, yours. Bugalwah is the point. Golden Ratio is what keeps it alive.
And the part that is not finished. Bugalwah has no practitioner names on it yet, no commissioned artwork, and the service design itself is still open. That is not us being coy. It is the work we want the lead clinician to do, and it is why the seat is worth taking rather than filling.
Part Two

We Would Pay a Recruiter $8,000 to Find You.
We Would Rather Pay You.

Same rate and same structure as we would have offered anyway. The $8,000 on top costs us exactly what finding you through an agency would have cost, so backing you is not a risk we are taking. It is the same cheque, pointed at the doctor instead of the middleman.

$8,000, split either side of a three-month trial. $4,000 the day you say yes. $4,000 at the end of three months, whether you stay or walk away. Not repayable, not clawed back, not conditional on signing anything, and on top of every hour you invoice.
Why we are doing it, plainly. Placing a doctor through a medical recruiter runs to about $8,000, and we would be handing that to a stranger for an introduction we already made ourselves. So it goes to you instead. It costs us the same either way, which is the whole point: nobody is doing anybody a favour here, and you are not being asked to take our word for anything. We want this moving in weeks rather than quarters, and this is us putting the money where our faith is. If you take the $8,000 and tell us in December it was not for you, that is a fair outcome and we will have paid for the answer.

$150 an Hour, Before the Clinic Earns a Cent

The hourly covers all of your clinical time: consultations, prescribing, TGA notifications, follow-ups and governance. It is paid whether the hour holds two patients or one very complicated one, and an hour that runs long is our cost to absorb, not yours. You are paid per consult attended, never per script written. A twenty minute conversation ending in "no, this is not right for you" pays exactly what a prescription does.

Then it climbs. Once the consults inside an hour have covered your base, you take the larger half of every consult after it and the clinic takes the smaller one. Here is the whole scale, with nothing held back for the call.
