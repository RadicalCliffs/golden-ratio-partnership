Anjouan is already the far edge of what still works. There is no cheaper licence worth holding and nothing greyer that a serious counterparty will accept. Take the direct licence, not a sublicence: a sublicence is cheaper up front and makes you a tenant of somebody else's compliance record, with no way to move your own payment relationships if theirs goes wrong.

One licence covers every vertical, so running a sportsbook only is a product decision rather than a saving — there's no cheaper single-vertical tier to drop to. Take the licence, ship the book, and keep casino as a switch you can flip later without going back to the regulator.

Where the grey is, and where it isn't

Fair game

  • Licensing in the cheapest jurisdiction that issues a real gaming licence
  • Zero local substance — no resident director, no island office, no staff on the ground
  • Paying 0% tax on gaming revenue, because that's the rate the jurisdiction sets
  • Geo-blocking every market you're not licensed for, instead of licensing market by market
  • Crypto-only deposits, which sidesteps card-scheme gatekeeping entirely and legitimately
  • A thin operating company that can be wound up and replaced without touching the group
  • Holding a licence that covers casino and poker too, and simply not switching them on
All of this is ordinary offshore structuring. It's how essentially every crypto book operates, and none of it needs hiding.

Hard lines

  • Taking players from a blocked market once you know they're there
  • Describing the business as something other than gambling to open a bank or processor account
  • Taking real money before the licence actually issues
  • Skipping age and identity checks, or running them as theatre
  • Leaving the structure off your own tax return at home
  • Marketing into a regulated market and calling it the affiliate's fault
These aren't grey areas with extra risk — they're the ones that end the business, and usually the balance with it. The route above is built so none of them is ever necessary.

What the licence asks of the platform

Being licensed in one place means declining everywhere you aren't, and that's a platform job we ship: geo-blocking on the United States, United Kingdom, Netherlands, France, Spain, Austria, Germany, Australia and Comoros, plus every FATF-blacklisted jurisdiction, with the list in config so it moves when the rules do. Two things operators routinely get wrong, and we wire both: IP blocking alone isn't enough — the ID document country, the payment method and the registration address all have to agree, or the control is decoration. And marketing is in scope — no traffic bought into a blocked market, affiliate terms that say so, with a clawback.

Three things to go in knowing

Anjouan is a two-to-three year position, not a permanent one. Gambling is prohibited under Comorian law at union level; Anjouan licenses under its own island legislation and the relationship between the two is unresolved. In practice it affects how third parties treat you more than the licence's day-to-day validity — but keep the structure portable and move to Curaçao once revenue covers it.

Mainstream payments will not touch an offshore book. Stripe, Adyen, PayPal, the card schemes — all no, on any of these licences. Build on iGaming specialists from day one and never plan around a mainstream processor changing its mind.

Your own tax residence follows the structure. An offshore operator doesn't make its profits untaxed to whoever controls it. Controlled-foreign-company rules in most countries can attribute that income to the owner personally whether or not it's ever distributed. Get it advised in your own jurisdiction before the company is formed, not after.

The bankroll is the real capital requirement, and no pricing page mentions it. A book has to pay every winner on the worst weekend of the season — before settlement, before the next deposit lands. Undercapitalised books don't fail because the software broke; they fail because a favourite-heavy Saturday cleaned out the float and withdrawals started queuing. In a crypto market, queued withdrawals kill a brand in about forty-eight hours, because the community talks. Budget the float first and treat it as untouchable. Everything above is the cheap part.
