How you get licensed. Or don't, yet.

You do not need your own gaming licence to launch. You need a provider who has one and will let you trade under it. That's stage one, and it's weeks rather than months. Stage two is when the economics improve and the obligations become yours.

Where the grey is, and where it isn't

Fair game

  • Trading under a provider's licence rather than buying your own on day one
  • Licensing later in the cheapest jurisdiction that issues a real gaming licence
  • Zero local substance — no resident director, no island office, no staff on the ground
  • Paying 0% tax on gaming revenue offshore, because that's the rate the jurisdiction sets
  • Geo-blocking every market you aren't licensed for, instead of licensing market by market
  • Crypto-first deposits, which sidesteps card-scheme gatekeeping entirely and legitimately
  • A thin operating company that can be wound up and replaced without touching the group
All of this is ordinary structuring. It's how most crypto books operate, and none of it needs hiding from anyone.

Hard lines

  • Taking players from a blocked market once you know they're there
  • Describing the business as something other than gambling to open a bank or processor account
  • Taking real money before the licence — yours or theirs — actually covers you
  • Skipping age and identity checks, or running them as theatre
  • Leaving the structure off your own tax return at home
  • Marketing into a regulated market and calling it the affiliate's fault
These aren't grey areas with extra risk. They're the ones that end the business, and usually the balance with it. The route above is built so none of them is ever necessary.
Two things to go in knowing

“Nobody enforces it” holds until you're worth enforcing against. In April 2026 Brazil blocked Polymarket and twenty-six other prediction sites — after the licensed sportsbook industry lobbied for it. It isn't only regulators who come for an unlicensed book taking share in their market. The incumbents file the complaint, and the regulator is the weapon. Polymarket's own answer was to buy a CFTC-licensed exchange for $112 million. Scale attracts the rules.

Your own tax residence follows the structure. An offshore operator doesn't make its profits untaxed to whoever controls it. Controlled-foreign-company rules in most countries can attribute that income to the owner personally whether or not it's ever distributed. Get it advised in your own jurisdiction before anything is incorporated — it's the highest-return money in the whole budget.
